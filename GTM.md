# Go-To-Market Strategy

The initial persona is a Series A engineering manager or technical founder at a 12-80 person B2B SaaS company that adopted AI tools quickly in 2024-2026 and now has messy renewals across Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT Business, Copilot, OpenAI API, and Gemini. This buyer is not looking for a generic "AI transformation" deck. They want a fast answer before a finance partner asks why developer tooling spend doubled.

The wedge is a two-minute public audit that produces one shareable URL and an honest threshold. The target user wants this right after Googling "ChatGPT Business pricing", "Cursor enterprise vs pro", "Claude Team minimum seats", "OpenAI API bill spike", or scrolling through an engineering Slack thread about AI tool renewals. If savings are above $500/month, the app pushes a Credex consultation. If savings are below $100/month, it says the team is spending well and offers a monitoring notification. That honesty is the trust mechanic. The product should feel more like a useful procurement calculator than lead bait.

Exact online hangouts for week one: `r/SaaS`, `r/startups`, `r/ExperiencedDevs`, `r/devops`, `r/ClaudeAI`, `r/GithubCopilot`, `r/cursor`, the Vercel community, Indie Hackers, Hacker News "Show HN", the Latent Space Discord, AI Engineer Discord, MLOps Community Slack, DevTools FM community, and founder/operator Slack groups where members discuss burn, credits, and tool consolidation. The first posts should not say "book a demo." They should lead with a teardown: "I priced the common AI tool stack for a 25-person startup and found the 2-seat team-plan trap."

The $0 distribution loop is: publish a pricing teardown, let operators run their own audit, generate a shareable URL, then ask them to reply with anonymized savings ranges. Each public reply becomes social proof for the next post. The strongest artifact is a weekly "AI Spend Waste Index" built from aggregated, non-sensitive audit outputs: median spend per employee, most common downgrade, average API leakage, and percentage of teams under the $100 savings threshold. Credex's unfair distribution channel is its access to real discounted AI-credit supply: it can turn an audit finding into an immediate credit-conversion conversation, while generic SaaS calculators can only recommend cancellation.

Week-one traction targets are intentionally concrete: 400 unique visitors, 120 completed audits, 45 email captures, 15 qualified leads above $500/month savings, 6 booked Credex consultations, and 2 credit-conversion opportunities. A secondary goal is qualitative: at least 10 comments from founders naming a missing tool, confusing billing behavior, or procurement objection. Those comments drive the next rules added to the audit engine.

The strongest outbound channel is founder-to-founder teardown. Pick 20 recently funded Series A companies from public funding announcements, inspect only public hiring/tooling signals, and send a short note: "Your team likely has at least three overlapping AI assistants. I made a calculator that checks whether the current mix is rational before renewal." No attachments, no scraped employee data, no fake personalization. The CTA is to run the audit, not to buy anything.
